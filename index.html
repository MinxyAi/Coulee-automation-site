What we do

Three ways we help.

No package tiers, no bundled hours you don't need — most engagements are one of these three, sized to the actual problem.

Build — custom software, built around how you actually work

We design and build focused tools for a specific job in your business — not a bloated platform, not a dashboard nobody opens. Our own home inspection reporting app started as exactly this kind of project: a tool built to solve one real problem well, from photo capture through to a client-ready report.

Consult — finding where AI actually helps

Most small businesses don't need "an AI strategy" — they need to know which two or three tasks are genuinely worth automating, and which aren't worth the trouble yet. We start with an honest look at your workflow, then hand you a short, realistic list of what's worth building or buying.

Train — getting your team comfortable with the tools you already have

A new tool only pays off if people actually use it. We run hands-on sessions with your team, working through your real day-to-day tasks with the AI tools you've already got or are considering — not a generic slideshow about "the future of AI."

August 2026

Why we built our own tool instead of buying one

We looked at existing home inspection software before writing a line of code, and most of it does a lot — more than a two-person crew doing walkthroughs actually needs. The features we wanted (a fast way to capture a finding, an AI draft we could review and correct, a report that looked like it came from a real business) kept getting buried under features we didn't.

So we built the smaller version instead. It's not trying to be everything — it's trying to be exactly enough, and nothing gets into a client's hands without a person checking it first.

August 2026

AI isn't magic — a practical starting point for small business owners

The most useful question we've found for a small business isn't "how do we use AI?" It's much narrower: what's one task, this week, that eats real time and follows a pattern? Writing up the same kind of report over and over. Drafting the same kind of email. Sorting through the same kind of note.

Start there, with something small enough to actually finish and check by hand. The bigger ideas can wait until the small one is working and you trust it.
