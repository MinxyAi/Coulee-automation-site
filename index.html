Questions we hear a lot

Straight answers, no sales pitch.

AI is still new territory for a lot of small businesses, and being unsure about it is a completely reasonable place to start. Here's what we tell people who ask.

What does this actually cost?

There's no fixed package price, because the honest answer depends on what you actually need — a two-week automation and a multi-month build cost very differently, and we'd rather scope to the real problem than sell you a bundle of hours you don't need. Pricing is always discussed upfront, in plain numbers, before any work starts, and sized to fit a small-business budget rather than an enterprise one. If something isn't worth the investment yet, we'll tell you that too.

How long does a project take?

Most of what we build runs weeks, not months — a focused tool solving one real problem doesn't need a six-month timeline, and we don't pad estimates to sound more impressive. You'll get a realistic timeline before we start, and an update if anything changes along the way, rather than silence until a deadline slips.

Why a small, family-run studio instead of a bigger firm?

Because you're always talking to the person actually doing the work, not an account manager relaying questions to someone else. We stay small by choice — it means lower overhead, more attention on each project we take on, and no incentive to sell you more than you need. What a bigger firm offers in scale, we make up for in actually being invested in getting it right for you.

We're hesitant about AI — will this really work for a business our size?

That hesitation is fair, and honestly a healthy instinct — AI is genuinely new territory for most small businesses, and a lot of the noise around it oversells what it can do. Our approach is to start small: one real, well-defined task, proven out before anything bigger gets discussed. If a tool doesn't earn its keep, we'll say so. We're not trying to sell you an "AI strategy" — we're trying to find the two or three places it actually saves your business real time.

Do we need to be technical to work with you?

No. Translating what's possible into plain language, and building something your team can actually use without a manual, is part of the job — it's also why training is one of our three core services, not an afterthought. If a tool needs a technical person standing behind it just to keep running, we haven't done our job.

What happens to our data if we work together?

We only collect what you actually share with us to do the work. We don't run analytics, ad trackers, or profiling cookies — that's not a compliance checkbox, it's in our Privacy Policy because we mean it. If you ever want to know exactly what we hold, just ask.

August 2026

Why we built our own tool instead of buying one

We looked at existing home inspection software before writing a line of code, and most of it does a lot — more than a two-person crew doing walkthroughs actually needs. The features we wanted (a fast way to capture a finding, an AI draft we could review and correct, a report that looked like it came from a real business) kept getting buried under features we didn't.

So we built the smaller version instead. It's not trying to be everything — it's trying to be exactly enough, and nothing gets into a client's hands without a person checking it first.

August 2026

AI isn't magic — a practical starting point for small business owners

The most useful question we've found for a small business isn't "how do we use AI?" It's much narrower: what's one task, this week, that eats real time and follows a pattern? Writing up the same kind of report over and over. Drafting the same kind of email. Sorting through the same kind of note.

Start there, with something small enough to actually finish and check by hand. The bigger ideas can wait until the small one is working and you trust it.
